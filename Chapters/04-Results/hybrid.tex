\subsection{Theoretical Hybrid Improvement}
\label{sec:results:hybrid}
An alternative to gadget decomposition often employed in \gls{rns} key-switching is the \textit{hybrid} technique \cite{cryptoeprint:2012/099, cryptoeprint:2021/204}. A natural question is wether this technique can be employed in the external and internal products.

% We defer a proper treatise of the 
In hybrid, the idea is to pre-scale a message $m$ by an auxiliary modulus $P$, where $|P| \approx |Q|/d_{num}$ for some integer $d_{num} | k$, resulting in a encryption of $m\cdot P$ in the extended ciphertext modulus $QP$. After some homomorphic operation(s) we end up with the message $m'P$ and more noise $\epsilon' \geq\epsilon$. Dividing the ciphertext by $P$ will result in a ciphertext encrypting $m'$ in modulus $Q$, but the noise is also scaled down by $P$.
\begin{gather*}
    (a\cdot s + \Delta m P + \epsilon, a) \pmod{QP} \\
    P^{-1}\cdot (a\cdot s + \Delta m' P + \epsilon', a) \pmod{QP} \\
    (a'\cdot s + \Delta m' + \epsilon'/P, a') \pmod{Q}
\end{gather*}

Reframing this technique in terms of the gadget framework established in \autoref{sec:gadget-decomposition} is straight forward. If we let $g = P$, the gadget property holds in the extended modulus $QP$. Using $P^{-1}$ as our decomposition is identical to dividing by $P$ in hybrid.
\begin{gather*}
    \langle x, yP\rangle = x\cdot y \cdot P \pmod{QP} \\
    \langle P^{-1}\cdot x, yP\rangle = P^{-1}\langle x, yP\rangle = x\cdot y \pmod{Q}
\end{gather*}

This is sufficient to define the external product using the hybrid gadget.\footnote{This result should come as no surprise since key-switching and the external product is essentially the same operation.} We must note, however, that this creates an imbalance between the left and right operands, where the right \gls{rgsw} must be defined in the extended modulus $QP$ while the left \gls{rlwe} is defined in the target modulus $Q$.

\begin{definition}[Hybrid-RGSW]
    An \gls{rgsw} ciphertext that encodes $\mu P$ \textbf{in the extended modulus} $QP$.
    \begin{equation}
        C_{QP} = Z_{QP} + \mu \begin{pmatrix}
            P & 0 \\
            0 & P
        \end{pmatrix} \pmod{QP}
    \end{equation}
    % \begin{equation}
    %     \Call{HybridRGSW}{b} = \Call{RGSW}{b\cdot P}
    % \end{equation}
\end{definition}
