Brakerski and Vaikuntanathan~\cite{cryptoeprint:2011/344} first decomposed ciphertext elements into small digits as a means of controlling the noise in their \emph{relinearization} technique. %% VERIFIED: BV11 applies the decomposition in relinearization / key switching, NOT in scalar multiplication. The "scalar multiplication" framing traces to the quoted survey line in 03-gadgets.tex, which describes gadget decomposition's general role rather than BV11's own contribution.
The idea has since been generalized into the idea of \emph{gadget decomposition} and employed in the design of numerous \gls{fhe} schemes % as an auxiliary tool for certain procedures, such as key-switching, or as the basis for entire scheme. 
\cite{cryptoeprint:2011/277, cryptoeprint:2012/078, cryptoeprint:2012/144, cryptoeprint:2013/340, cryptoeprint:2014/816, cryptoeprint:2016/421, cryptoeprint:2018/421}.

\begin{definition}[Digit Decomposition]
    \label{def:digit-decomposition}
    Given an integer $\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_q$ for a modulus $q\geq 2$, base $B$ and decomposition parameter $\ell$, the digit decomposition $\decomp{\gamma}{} = (\gamma_0, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_{\ell-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}_B^\ell$ outputs an ordered list of $\ell$ base-$B$ \emph{digits} satisfying
    \begin{equation*}
        \gamma = \gamma_0\frac{q}{B} + \gamma_1\frac{q}{B^2} + ... \gamma_{\ell - 1}\frac{q}{B^{\ell}} = \sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\gamma_i\frac{q}{B^{i+1}}
    \end{equation*}
\end{definition}

%%
%% Why its helpful
Digit decomposition has two important properties. First, if the digits $\gamma_i$ of $\decomp{\gamma}{}$ are small, then $\langle \decomp{\gamma}{}, \epsilon\rangle \leq \ell\cdot \max\{\gamma_i\cdot \epsilon_i\} \leq \ell B\epsilon \ll \gamma\epsilon$. This is the property that allows some control over the noise growth. Second, two integers can be multiplied through the decomposition. By construction, $\langle\decomp{\gamma}{}, g\rangle = \gamma$ for the fixed vector $g = (q/B, \dots, q/B^{\ell})$, meaning $\langle\decomp{\gamma}{}, g\cdot x\rangle = \gamma\cdot x$ for an arbitrary integer $x\in \mathbb{Z}$. For either of these properties, the particular form of $g$ is unimportant as long as the digits $\gamma_i$ remain small. Furthermore, as we are generally working with some intrinsic error in FHE, \cite{cryptoeprint:2018/421} proposed allowing some error $\epsilon$ in the reconstruction, giving the definition from their paper verbatim:

%% Gadget Decomposition

\begin{definition}[(Abstract) Gadget Decomposition]
    \label{def:tfhe:gadget-decomposition}
    An efficient algorithm $\decomp{v}{g,\beta,\epsilon}$ is a valid decomposition on the gadget $g\in \mathbb{Z}_q^\ell$ with quality $\beta \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$ and precision $\epsilon \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ if and only if for any ring element $v\in R_Q$ it efficiently and publicly outputs a small vector $u \in \mathbb{Z}_q$ such that $\|u\|_\infty \leq \beta$ and $\langle u, g\rangle \leq v + \epsilon$.

    % Furthermore, the expectation of u · H − v must to be equal to 0 when v is uniformly distributed in M .
\end{definition}

The entries of the output vector $u$ are again called \emph{digits}, named after digit decomposition, though for a general gadget they need not be digits of any base. It is convenient to name the two sides of the reconstruction rather than to carry an algorithm and a fixed gadget separately. Writing $D(v)$ for the output of $\decomp{v}{g,\beta,\epsilon}$ and $P(u)$ for the gadget-scaled vector $g\cdot u$, the condition $\langle u, g\rangle \leq v + \epsilon$ becomes a statement about a pair of vectors, which is the form used throughout this thesis.

\begin{definition}[Gadget Property]
    Two vectors $P(x) \in \mathbb{Z}_Q^\ell$ and $D(y) \in \mathbb{Z}_\beta^\ell$ are said to have the \textit{gadget property} if and only if they satisfy \eqref{def:gadget-property} for any pair of ring elements $(x,y)\in R_Q^2$ with precision $\epsilon \in \mathbb{Z}_q^+$ and quality $\beta$ where $\|D(y)\|_\infty \leq \beta$. If $\epsilon = 0$ then $P(x)$ and $D(y)$ are said to have the \textit{exact} gadget property.
    \begin{equation}
        \label{def:gadget-property}
        \langle P(x), D(y)\rangle = x\cdot y + \epsilon \pmod{Q}
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Layered Gadget Decomposition]
    \label{thm:gadget-layers}
    Let $(P_1, D_1)$ and $(P_2, D_2)$ be two pairs with the gadget property, of quality $\beta_1, \beta_2$ and precision $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2$ respectively. Their composition, defined component-wise by $(P_1\circ P_2)(u) = P_1(P_2(u))$ and $(D_1\circ D_2)(v) = D_1(D_2(v))$, again has the gadget property, with quality $\beta_1$ and precision $\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2$.
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:gadget-layers}
        \langle (P_1\circ P_2)(u),\; (D_1\circ D_2)(v)\rangle = u\cdot v + \epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 \pmod{Q}
    \end{equation}

    \begin{proof}
        Apply the gadget property of the outer pair to the vectors produced by the inner one, then the gadget property of the inner pair.
        \begin{gather*}
            \langle P_1(P_2(u)),\; D_1(D_2(v)) \rangle = \langle P_2(u),\; D_2(v)\rangle + \epsilon_1 = u\cdot v + \epsilon_2 + \epsilon_1 \pmod{Q}
        \end{gather*}
        The digits of the composition are those produced last, by $D_1$, so the quality is the quality $\beta_1$ of the outer decomposition.
    \end{proof}
\end{lemma}

\subsubsection{RNS Instantiation}
\label{sec:hps}
Digit decomposition cannot be applied directly to ring elements in an \gls{rns} scheme where the system only has access to the residues. When Bajard et al. implemented the first \gls{rns} version of the BV scheme \cite{cryptoeprint:2016/510} they exchanged digit decomposition for an \gls{rns} decomposition, exploiting the fact that this decomposition is already required by the representation. Halevi, Polyakov and Shoup later simplified their construction into the form below \cite{cryptoeprint:2018/117}, which is the one used here.

\begin{definition}[HPS Gadget]
    Recall from~\autoref{sec:rns-base-conversion} the factors $\hat{q}_i = Q/q_i$, and let $[\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i}$ denote the modular inverse of $\hat{q}_i$ so that $\hat{q}_i\cdot[\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i} \equiv 1 \pmod{q_i}$. By formulating the \gls{crt} as an inner product \eqref{eq:crt-gadget}, it is immediately clear that the \gls{rns} decomposition with moduli $q_i$ is an exact decomposition on $g = (\hat{q}_0\cdot[\hat{q}_0^{-1}]_{q_0}, \dots, \hat{q}_{k-1}\cdot[\hat{q}_{k-1}^{-1}]_{q_{k-1}})$ with $\beta = \max_i\{q_i\}/2$ (assuming centered residues).
    \begin{align}
        \label{def:hps:P}
        \hps{P}(a) &= (a\cdot[\hat{q}^{-1}_0]_{q_0}\cdot \hat{q}_0, \dots, a\cdot[\hat{q}^{-1}_{k-1}]_{q_{k-1}}\cdot [\hat{q}_{k-1}]_Q) \\
        \label{def:hps:D}
        \hps{D}(a) &= ([a]_{q_0}, [a]_{q_1}, \dots, [a]_{q_{k-1}})
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:hps-optimization}
    As \gls{rns} stores the polynomial $a\in R_Q$ as the set of residues $[a]_{q_i}$ for all $q_i \in \mathcal{B}_{Q}$, the $\hps{D}(\cdot)$ vector \eqref{def:hps:D} is \emph{free} in the sense that calculating $\hps{D}(a) \cong a$ requires no arithmetic. In fact, neither does \eqref{def:hps:P} since $\hat{q}_i = Q/q_i = (\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}q_l)/q_i$ contains $q_j$ as a factor unless $i=j$, in which case $\hat{q}_i\cdot[\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i} = 1$ by definition! \\
    \begin{gather*}
        a\cdot[\hat{q}^{-1}_i]_{q_i}\cdot\hat{q}_i = \{(a\cdot[\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i}\cdot\hat{q}_i) \bmod{q_j} \}_{j = 0}^{k-1} 
        \\
        % \text{note that } \hat{q}_i = \frac{1}{q_i}\cdot\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}q_l \text{ contains } q_j \text{ as a factor} 
        % \\
        % \text{ unless } i = j \text{, in which case } \hat{q}_i\cdot\hat{q}_i^{-1} = 1 \text{ by definition}
        % \\
        % \therefore\; 
        (a\cdot[\hat{q}^{-1}_i]_{q_i}\cdot\hat{q}_i) \bmod q_j = 
        \begin{cases}
            0, & i \not= j\\
            [a]_{q_j}, & i = j
        \end{cases}
    \end{gather*}
\end{remark} 

While the HPS gadget is cheap, its quality $\beta = \max_i\{q_i\}/2$ is too large for many applications, as the digits are the full residues themselves. A second layer of decomposition brings the quality down. Every digit of $\hps{D}(a)$ fits inside a machine word, since the \gls{rns} moduli are chosen that way, so the digit decomposition of \autoref{def:digit-decomposition} applies to each limb directly and \autoref{thm:gadget-layers} makes the composition a valid gadget in its own right. Taking the second layer on the powers of a base $B$, that is on $g = (1, B, \dots, B^{\ell-1})$, splits each of the $k$ residues into $\ell$ digits. The composed gadget produces $k\ell$ digits of quality $\beta = B/2$ once they are centered, and it is exact whenever $B^\ell \geq \max_i\{q_i\}$, which fixes the decomposition parameter at $\ell = \lceil \log_B \max_i\{q_i\}\rceil$.
