\section{Notation}
\label{sec:notation}

The following conventions hold throughout this thesis. All logarithms are base $2$, so that $\log x$ abbreviates $\log_2 x$. Ring elements, vectors and matrices are denoted by plain italic letters, and their type is stated at first use. For a vector $v$, both $v_i$ and $v[i]$ denote the $i$-th component, and a sequence $(f(0), \dots, f(k-1))$ of $k$ elements is abbreviated $\{f(i)\}_{i=0}^{k-1}$. The norm $\|\cdot\|$ is always the infinity norm $\|v\|_\infty = \max_i |v_i|$, the largest absolute value among the components of a vector or, for a polynomial, among its coefficients. Asymptotic costs are given in the usual $\mathcal{O}(\cdot)$ notation and count homomorphic operations or transmitted ciphertexts rather than machine instructions; the unit is named wherever such a bound is stated.

\begin{definition}[Centered Residues]
    \label{def:centered}
    Let $\mathbb{Z}_Q = \mathbb{Z}/Q\mathbb{Z}$ denote the ring of integers modulo $Q \geq 2$. While any set of residue class representatives may stand for $\mathbb{Z}_Q$, this work canonically identifies $\mathbb{Z}_Q$ with the $Q$ consecutive integers centered at the origin, selected by the following map.
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:centered}
        [a]_Q = \big(\, (a + \lfloor Q/2 \rfloor) \bmod Q \,\big) - \lfloor Q/2 \rfloor
    \end{equation}
    The flooring makes the map well defined for every modulus, and every representative satisfies $|[a]_Q| \leq \lfloor Q/2 \rfloor$. For example, $\mathbb{Z}_5 = \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$. Applied to a vector or polynomial, $[\cdot]_Q$ acts on each component or coefficient. The size of an element modulo $Q$ always refers to the norm of its centered representative lifted to $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{Z}[X]$; it is this convention that bounds quantities by $Q/2$ rather than by $Q$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Working Ring]
    \label{def:ring}
    All plaintext and ciphertext spaces in this work are cyclotomic quotient rings of the form $R_Q = \mathbb{Z}_Q[X]/(X^N + 1)$, where the dimension $N$ is a power of two and $Q$ is a modulus. An element $a \in R_Q$ is a polynomial of degree at most $N - 1$ with centered coefficients, written in the \emph{canonical form} \eqref{form:canonical} or the equivalent \emph{coefficient form} \eqref{form:coefficient}.
    \begin{align}
        \label{form:canonical}
        a &= c_0 + c_1X + \cdots + c_{N-1}X^{N-1} = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} c_iX^i \\
        \label{form:coefficient}
        a &= (c_0, c_1, \dots, c_{N-1}) = \{c_i\}_{i=0}^{k-1}
    \end{align}
\end{definition}
