\subsection{Base Conversion}
\label{sec:rns-base-conversion}
Several FHE procedures require changing the set of moduli a value is represented in: extending the basis before operations that need headroom, and reducing it again to manage noise. This subsection defines base conversion and the operations derived from it in the residue setting.

\begin{definition}[RNS Basis]
    Given a modulus $Q = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} q_i$ composed of $k$ pairwise coprime factors, the \textit{RNS basis} $\mathcal{B}_Q = \{q_0, q_1, \dots, q_{k-1}\}$ is the set of moduli used in the CRT representation \eqref{form:crt}:
    \begin{equation} %% TODO: This is a neat formula but it doesn't fit here at all, is more applicable to RNS in general or CRT
        \mathbb{Z}_Q \cong \mathbb{Z}_{q_0} \times \mathbb{Z}_{q_1} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{Z}_{q_{k-1}}
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

%% TODO: Denote this as abuse of notation or formally "extend the notation of~\autoref{def:centered} to sets" or bases
%% TODO: This whole paragraph is awkward, it does too much: extends notation, defines notation of CRT and evaluation form in one sentence 
The centered modulo notation of \autoref{def:centered} extends to bases: $[a]_{\mathcal{B}_Q} = ([a]_{q_0}, \dots, [a]_{q_{k-1}})$ denotes the CRT form \eqref{form:crt} of $a$, and $\ntt{[a]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}}$ its evaluation form \eqref{form:eval}.

\begin{definition}[Base Conversion]
    Let $\mathcal{B}_Q = \{q_0,\dots,q_{k-1}\}$ and
    $\mathcal{B} = \{b_0,\dots,b_{k'-1}\}$ be RNS bases with
    $\gcd(Q, B) = 1$, where $B = \prod_{j} b_j$. Base conversion is
    the map that takes the representation of $x$ in $\mathcal{B}_Q$
    to the representation of $[x]_Q$ in $\mathcal{B}$, i.e.
    \begin{equation*}
        \left([x]_{q_0}, \dots, [x]_{q_{k-1}}\right)
        \;\longmapsto\;
        \left([x]_{b_0}, \dots, [x]_{b_{k'-1}}\right)
    \end{equation*}
    When the residues in $\mathcal{B}$ are appended to those in $\mathcal{B}_Q$, so that $x$ becomes represented in the \textit{extended} basis $\mathcal{B}_Q \cup \mathcal{B}$ for the modulus $QB$, the operation is also referred to as \emph{base extension}.
\end{definition}

The naive approach to base conversion reconstructs $x$ from its residues through the inverse \gls{crt}, and then reduces the result against each modulus of the new basis. This reintroduces exactly the multi-precision arithmetic that \gls{rns} was meant to avoid; the remainder of this section covers the cheaper, approximate alternative and the operations built on top of it.

\subsubsection{Fast Base Conversion}
Write $\hat{q}_i = Q/q_i$ for the CRT factors of the basis $\mathcal{B}_Q$, and $[\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i}$ for their modular inverses. Introduced in \cite{cryptoeprint:2016/510}, fast base conversion operates directly on the residues, avoiding the CRT reconstruction and multi-precision integers entirely.
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:fastbconv}
    \Call{FastBConv}{x, \mathcal{B}_Q, \mathcal{B}} = \left\{\left[\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left[x_i\, \hat{q}_i^{-1}\right]_{q_i} \cdot \hat{q}_i\right]_b\right\}_{b\in \mathcal{B}}
\end{equation} %% TODO: Computing \alpha exactly or bounding it to {-1, 0, 1}, I think they showed how to do in that paper. Verify and write into single sentence.
The price is approximation: because the sum is never reduced modulo $Q$, \eqref{eq:fastbconv} produces not $[x]_Q$ but $[x]_Q + \alpha_x Q$ for some integer $\alpha_x \in [0, k)$. Computing $\alpha_x$ exactly is possible but requires costly operations in \gls{rns} \cite{cryptoeprint:2018/117}, and is unnecessary when converting as part of an \textit{extension}. Note also that \eqref{eq:fastbconv} operates on the CRT form: an operand held in evaluation form must first be brought back with the inverse \gls{ntt}, which is part of the true cost of the conversion.

\subsubsection{Fast Base Extension} %% TODO: Define "$P$-towers" formally: the towers $\bmod{p_i}$ where $p_i \in \mathcal{B}_P$
Base extension only requires the new residues, so \eqref{eq:fastbconv} can be used as a subroutine: the $\mathcal{B}_P$-residues are computed with $\Call{FastBConv}{x, \mathcal{B}_Q, \mathcal{B}_P}$, and since $\mathcal{B}_{QP} = \mathcal{B}_Q \cup \mathcal{B}_P$ the original residues are kept unchanged.

%%%
%%% Fast Base Extension
%%%
\begin{algorithm}[ht!]
\caption{Fast Base Extension}
\label{alg:fast_base_extension}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require $\ntt{[a]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}}$ and a basis $\mathcal{B}_P$
    \Ensure $\ntt{[a + \alpha_aQ]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}}}$ for some $\alpha_a\in[0, k)$
    \State $a_{Q} \gets \Call{InverseNTT}{a}$
    \State $\mathsf{conv} \gets \sum_{i = 0}^{k-1} [a_Q[i]\cdot\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i}\cdot \hat{q}_i$
    \Comment{Fast Base Conversion}
    \For{$p \in \mathcal{B}_P$}
        \State $a \gets a \cup \Call{NTT}{[\mathsf{conv}]_p}$
    \EndFor
    \State\Return $a$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:fbe-exact-extension}
    Fast base conversion is approximate as a \textit{conversion} but exact as an \textit{extension}: because $\alpha_x Q \equiv 0 \pmod{q_i}$, the reused $Q$-residues remain consistent, and the extended vector is the exact RNS representation of the lifted integer $x' = [x]_Q + \alpha_x Q \in \mathbb{Z}_{QP}$. The operations below are designed around this overflow, either cancelling it outright or shrinking it to a small additive term.
\end{remark}

    The factors $\hat{q}_i$ and $[\hat{q}^{-1}_i]_{q_i}$ depend only on the basis, and can be pre-calculated and stored in memory (discussed in \autoref{sec:impl:core}).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Scale}
The overflow $\alpha_xQ$ produced by \autoref{alg:fast_base_extension} is removed completely if the result is multiplied by $P$:
\begin{gather*}
    x' = x + \alpha_xQ \pmod{QP} \\
    x'P = xP + \alpha_x QP = xP \pmod{QP}
\end{gather*}
This observation leads to the specialized operation ``\textit{extend and scale by} $P$''. I refer to this operation as $\Call{Scale}{x, P} \mapsto [xP]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}}$.
\begin{equation}
    \label{def:scale}
    \Call{Scale}{x, P} = \Call{FastBaseExtend}{x, \mathcal{B}_Q, \mathcal{B}_P} \cdot P
\end{equation}

In \gls{rns} the operation is far cheaper than \eqref{def:scale} suggests. Every $P$-residue of $xP$ is $0$ modulo $p_i$, so no conversion is needed at all: each $Q$-tower is multiplied by $[P]_{q_i}$, and $k'$ empty towers are appended (\autoref{alg:scale}). In particular, \textsc{Scale} avoids the \gls{ntt} conversions of the fast base extension entirely and operates directly on the evaluation form.

\begin{algorithm}[ht!]
\caption{\protect\Call{Scale}{x, P}}
\label{alg:scale}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require $\ntt{[x]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}}$ with $|\mathcal{B}_{Q}| = k$ and a basis $\mathcal{B}_P$ with $|\mathcal{B}_{P}| = k'$
    \Ensure $\ntt{[xP]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}}}$
    \For{$(x_i, q_i)\in x$}
        \State $x_i \gets x_i \cdot P \bmod{q_i}$
    \EndFor
    \For{$i \in [0, k')$}
        \State $x_{k + i} \gets 0 \bmod{p_i}$
        \Comment{Append empty $p_i$-tower to $x$}
    \EndFor
    \State\Return $x$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Modulus Switching}
%% TODO: Poorly written and sounds like AI. Rewrite entire (subsub)section. 
Rounding is not natively supported in \gls{rns}: the residues are independent and non-positional, so the magnitude of a value --- and with it any fractional correction --- is inaccessible without CRT reconstruction. The modulus switch of \autoref{def:modswitch}, scaling a ciphertext element $c$ from $Q$ to $Q' = Q/q_{k-1}$, is instead realized by \textit{dropping the last limb} and applying a small correction to each remaining limb \cite{cryptoeprint:2021/204}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rns-modswitch}
    [c']_{q_i} = \left[q_{k-1}^{-1}\left(c - \delta\right)\right]_{q_i}, \quad 0 \leq i < k - 1
\end{equation}
where the correction $\delta \equiv c \pmod{q_{k-1}}$ replicates the rounding and, for BGV, must additionally satisfy $\delta \equiv 0 \pmod{t}$ so that the plaintext is preserved. The noise shrinks by a factor $q_{k-1}$, matching the classical modulus switch. %% TODO: Remove BGV stuff, we don't really care.

\subsubsection{Approximate Mod Down}
Switching from the extended modulus $QP$ back down to $Q$ faces the same obstacle: dividing by $P$ requires rounding, and thus a correction congruent to $x$ modulo $P$. The correction is obtained by converting the $P$-part of $x$ back to $\mathcal{B}_Q$ with \eqref{eq:fastbconv} \cite{cryptoeprint:2012/099, cryptoeprint:2018/117}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:approxmoddown}
    \Call{ApproxModDown}{x} = \left[P^{-1}\left(x - \Call{FastBConv}{[x]_{\mathcal{B}_P}, \mathcal{B}_P, \mathcal{B}_Q}\right)\right]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}
\end{equation}
The inner conversion carries its own overflow $\alpha P$, which survives the division as a small additive error $\alpha \in [0, k')$ --- hence \textit{approximate} mod down. In typical use the operand is pre-multiplied by $P$, by \textsc{Scale} or by $P$-scaled key material, so the division returns the value to its original scale while shrinking the noise accumulated at modulus $QP$ by a factor $P$; this is what makes the approximation tolerable in practice. For BGV the converted term must additionally be corrected modulo $t$ to preserve the plaintext \cite{cryptoeprint:2021/204}.
