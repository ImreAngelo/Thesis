\subsection{Base Conversion}
\label{sec:rns-base-conversion}
The evaluation form \eqref{form:eval} makes addition and multiplication cheap, but pins the modulus: the residues are independent and non-positional, so the magnitude of a value --- and with it division and rounding, the operations noise management is made of --- is inaccessible without \gls{crt} reconstruction. Changing the modulus fares no better, as a representation in $\mathcal{B}_Q$ says nothing about a value modulo anything outside $\mathcal{B}_Q$. This subsection recovers both.

The operations are best understood through the pattern they serve: the temporary modulus extension introduced for key switching (\autoref{sec:gadget-decomposition}) by Gentry--Halevi--Smart \cite{cryptoeprint:2012/099}. A value is raised from $Q$ to an extended modulus $QP$, multiplied there against operands carrying a factor $P$, and brought back down by a division by $P$: the payload returns to its original scale, while the noise of the intermediate computation shrinks by the same factor. One primitive and three derived operations realize the trip.

\begin{center}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
        \toprule
        Operation & Computes & Error \\
        \midrule
        \textsc{FastBConv}      & $[x]_{\mathcal{B}_Q} \mapsto [x + \alpha_x Q]_{\mathcal{B}}$      & exact for the lift $x + \alpha_x Q$ \\
        \textsc{FastBaseExtend} & $[x]_{\mathcal{B}_Q} \mapsto [x + \alpha_x Q]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}}$ & exact, as an extension \\
        \textsc{Scale}          & $[x]_{\mathcal{B}_Q} \mapsto [xP]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}}$             & exact \\
        \textsc{ApproxModDown}  & $[x]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}} \mapsto [\lfloor x/P \rceil - \alpha]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}$ & additive, small $\alpha$ \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{definition}[RNS Basis]
    Given a modulus $Q = \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} q_i$ composed of $k$ pairwise coprime factors, the \textit{RNS basis} $\mathcal{B}_Q = \{q_0, q_1, \dots, q_{k-1}\}$ is the set of moduli underlying the CRT form \eqref{form:crt} of $\mathbb{Z}_Q$.
\end{definition}

By a slight abuse of notation, the centered representation of \autoref{def:centered} extends to bases: $[a]_{\mathcal{B}_Q} = ([a]_{q_0}, \dots, [a]_{q_{k-1}})$ denotes the CRT form \eqref{form:crt} of $a$, and $\ntt{[a]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}}$ its evaluation form \eqref{form:eval}.

\begin{definition}[Base Conversion]
    Let $\mathcal{B}_Q = \{q_0,\dots,q_{k-1}\}$ and
    $\mathcal{B} = \{b_0,\dots,b_{k'-1}\}$ be RNS bases with
    $\gcd(Q, B) = 1$, where $B = \prod_{j} b_j$. Base conversion is
    the map that takes the representation of $x$ in $\mathcal{B}_Q$
    to the representation of $[x]_Q$ in $\mathcal{B}$, i.e.
    \begin{equation*}
        \left([x]_{q_0}, \dots, [x]_{q_{k-1}}\right)
        \;\longmapsto\;
        \left([x]_{b_0}, \dots, [x]_{b_{k'-1}}\right)
    \end{equation*}
    When the residues in $\mathcal{B}$ are appended to those in $\mathcal{B}_Q$, so that $x$ becomes represented in the \textit{extended} basis $\mathcal{B}_Q \cup \mathcal{B}$ for the modulus $QB$, the operation is also referred to as a \emph{base extension}.
\end{definition}

\subsubsection{Fast Base Conversion}
The naive conversion reconstructs $x$ from its residues through the inverse \gls{crt} and reduces the result against each modulus of the new basis --- reintroducing exactly the multi-precision arithmetic that \gls{rns} exists to avoid. Fast base conversion \cite{cryptoeprint:2016/510} stays on the residues. Writing $\hat{q}_i = Q/q_i$ for the CRT factors of $\mathcal{B}_Q$, $[\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i}$ for their modular inverses and $x_i = [x]_{q_i}$ for the residues of the operand,
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:fastbconv}
    \Call{FastBConv}{x, \mathcal{B}_Q, \mathcal{B}} = \left\{\left[\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left[x_i\, \hat{q}_i^{-1}\right]_{q_i} \cdot \hat{q}_i\right]_b\right\}_{b\in \mathcal{B}}
\end{equation}

The sum in \eqref{eq:fastbconv} is never reduced modulo $Q$; it evaluates to the integer
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:fastbconv-lift}
    \tilde{x} \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left[x_i\, \hat{q}_i^{-1}\right]_{q_i} \cdot \hat{q}_i \;=\; [x]_Q + \alpha_x Q,
    \qquad |\tilde{x}| \leq \tfrac{k}{2}\,Q,
\end{equation}
since $\tilde{x} \equiv x \pmod{q_i}$ for every $q_i \in \mathcal{B}_Q$ while each centered digit is bounded by $q_i/2$. \textsc{FastBConv} therefore computes the $\mathcal{B}$-residues of the \emph{lift} $\tilde{x}$ exactly, and is approximate only as a conversion of $x$: the output represents $[x]_Q + \alpha_x Q$ for an unknown overflow $\alpha_x \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $|\alpha_x| \leq \lceil k/2 \rceil$. Recovering $\alpha_x$, and with it the exact conversion, is possible at additional cost \cite{cryptoeprint:2018/117}; none of the operations built on the primitive requires it.

The conversion is \emph{fast} under one standing assumption and one caveat. The assumption: the constants $[\hat{q}_i]_b$ and $[\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i}$ depend only on the two bases, and are precomputed once and stored (\autoref{sec:impl:core}); what remains at runtime is $k \cdot k'$ single-word multiply-accumulates per coefficient. The caveat: \eqref{eq:fastbconv} operates on the CRT form, so an operand held in evaluation form must first be brought back with the inverse \gls{ntt} --- in practice the dominant cost of a conversion.

\subsubsection{Fast Base Extension}
Let $\mathcal{B}_P = \{p_0, \dots, p_{k'-1}\}$ be a second basis with every modulus coprime to those of $\mathcal{B}_Q$, and write $\mathcal{B}_{QP} = \mathcal{B}_Q \cup \mathcal{B}_P$ for the extended basis. Raising $x$ from $\mathbb{Z}_Q$ to $\mathbb{Z}_{QP}$ requires only the new residues: the $Q$-limbs are kept unchanged, and the $P$-limbs --- the limbs modulo the $p_j \in \mathcal{B}_P$ --- are produced by $\Call{FastBConv}{x, \mathcal{B}_Q, \mathcal{B}_P}$ (\autoref{alg:fast_base_extension}).

%%%
%%% Fast Base Extension
%%%
\begin{algorithm}[ht!]
\caption{Fast Base Extension}
\label{alg:fast_base_extension}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require $\ntt{[a]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}}$ and a basis $\mathcal{B}_P$
    \Ensure $\ntt{[a + \alpha_aQ]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}}}$ for some $\alpha_a \in \mathbb{Z}$, $|\alpha_a| \leq \lceil k/2 \rceil$
    \State $a_{Q} \gets \Call{InverseNTT}{a}$
    \State $\mathsf{conv} \gets \sum_{i = 0}^{k-1} [a_Q[i]\cdot\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i}\cdot \hat{q}_i$
    \Comment{Fast Base Conversion}
    \For{$p \in \mathcal{B}_P$}
        \State $a \gets a \cup \Call{NTT}{[\mathsf{conv}]_p}$
    \EndFor
    \State\Return $a$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Exactness as an extension]
    \label{prop:fbe-exact}
    The output of \autoref{alg:fast_base_extension} is the exact RNS representation in $\mathcal{B}_{QP}$ of the lifted integer $\tilde{x} = [x]_Q + \alpha_x Q \in \mathbb{Z}_{QP}$: the appended $P$-limbs are those of $\tilde{x}$ by \eqref{eq:fastbconv-lift}, and the reused $Q$-limbs agree with $\tilde{x}$ because $\tilde{x} \equiv x \pmod{q_i}$.
\end{proposition}

The approximation of the primitive is thus not an error in any residue, but a change of representative: the extension represents, exactly, an integer that is congruent to $x$ modulo $Q$ and bounded by $\frac{k}{2}Q$.

\subsubsection{Scale}
The raising leg of the modulus extension needs operands that carry the factor $P$: whatever is meant to survive the final division must be $P$-scaled before it. I refer to the operation supplying them, ``\textit{extend and scale by} $P$'', as $\Call{Scale}{x, P} \mapsto [xP]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}}$. Composing the extension with a multiplication defines it,
\begin{equation}
    \label{def:scale}
    \Call{Scale}{x, P} = \Call{FastBaseExtend}{x, \mathcal{B}_Q, \mathcal{B}_P} \cdot P
\end{equation}
and the composition is exact even though its ingredient is not, because multiples of $Q$ are annihilated by $P$ in $\mathbb{Z}_{QP}$:
\begin{equation*}
    (x + \alpha_x Q) \cdot P = xP + \alpha_x QP \equiv xP \pmod{QP}
\end{equation*}

In \gls{rns} the operation is far cheaper than \eqref{def:scale} suggests: no conversion needs to take place at all. Every $P$-limb of $xP$ is zero --- each $p_j$ divides $P$ --- so the limbs that the extension would have to compute are known in advance. Each $Q$-limb is multiplied by $[P]_{q_i}$ and $k'$ zero limbs are appended (\autoref{alg:scale}); \textsc{Scale} never leaves the evaluation form, and is limb-local, \gls{ntt}-free and exact.

\begin{algorithm}[ht!]
\caption{\protect\Call{Scale}{x, P}}
\label{alg:scale}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require $\ntt{[x]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}}$ with $|\mathcal{B}_{Q}| = k$ and a basis $\mathcal{B}_P$ with $|\mathcal{B}_{P}| = k'$
    \Ensure $\ntt{[xP]_{\mathcal{B}_{QP}}}$
    \For{$(x_i, q_i)\in x$}
        \State $x_i \gets x_i \cdot P \bmod{q_i}$
    \EndFor
    \For{$i \in [0, k')$}
        \State $x_{k + i} \gets 0 \bmod{p_i}$
        \Comment{Append empty $p_i$-tower to $x$}
    \EndFor
    \State\Return $x$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:scale-collapse}
    Read right to left, \eqref{def:scale} states that a multiplication by $P$ collapses the two raising operations into one: $P$-scaling the output of \autoref{alg:fast_base_extension} yields the same element of $\mathbb{Z}_{QP}$ as \textsc{Scale}, with the overflow annihilated en route.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Modulus Switching}
The modulus switch of \autoref{def:modswitch} scales a ciphertext element by $Q'/Q$ and rounds --- a positional operation, and the first casualty of the residue representation. Realized subtractively, however, it needs no positions: for the switch from $Q$ to $Q' = Q/q_{k-1}$, a correction $\delta \equiv c \pmod{q_{k-1}}$ makes $c - \delta$ an exact multiple of $q_{k-1}$, the division becomes a modular multiplication by $[q_{k-1}^{-1}]_{q_i}$ on each remaining limb, and the last limb --- identically zero after the subtraction --- is dropped \cite{cryptoeprint:2021/204}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:rns-modswitch}
    [c']_{q_i} = \left[q_{k-1}^{-1}\left(c - \delta\right)\right]_{q_i}, \quad 0 \leq i < k - 1
\end{equation}
The correction is read directly off the limb being dropped: with the centered representative $\delta = [c]_{q_{k-1}}$, subtracting $\delta$ moves $c$ to the nearest multiple of $q_{k-1}$, and \eqref{eq:rns-modswitch} computes exactly $\lfloor c/q_{k-1} \rceil$. The noise shrinks by the same factor, matching \autoref{def:modswitch}.

\subsubsection{Approximate Mod Down}
The descent of the modulus extension is the same switch performed from $QP$ down to $Q$, dropping all of $\mathcal{B}_P$ at once. The correction must now be congruent to $x$ modulo the composite $P$, and it is already present in the operand: the $P$-limbs hold precisely $[x]_{\mathcal{B}_P}$. What the single-limb switch could read off directly must here be carried across bases --- a base conversion \cite{cryptoeprint:2012/099, cryptoeprint:2018/117}:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:approxmoddown}
    \Call{ApproxModDown}{x} = \left[P^{-1}\left(x - \Call{FastBConv}{[x]_{\mathcal{B}_P}, \mathcal{B}_P, \mathcal{B}_Q}\right)\right]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}
\end{equation}
The conversion being the fast one, the subtracted term is not $[x]_P$ but a lift $[x]_P + \alpha P$ with $|\alpha| \leq \lceil k'/2 \rceil$ \eqref{eq:fastbconv-lift}, and the extra multiple of $P$ survives the division as an additive error on the result --- hence \emph{approximate}:
\begin{equation*}
    \Call{ApproxModDown}{x} \equiv \left\lfloor x/P \right\rceil - \alpha \pmod{Q}
\end{equation*}

The error exchange is what the temporary extension exists to buy. Everything of value at modulus $QP$ carries a factor $P$ --- put there by \textsc{Scale} or by $P$-scaled key material --- so the operand arriving at the descent has the form $x = Py + e$, with $e$ the noise deposited by the computation at $QP$. \textsc{ApproxModDown} returns
\begin{equation*}
    y + \lfloor e/P \rceil - \alpha \pmod{Q}
\end{equation*}
The payload lands at its original scale, the noise arrives divided by $P$, and the price is an additive term of a few integers. For BGV the subtraction must additionally not disturb the plaintext residue, so the converted correction is chosen congruent to $0$ modulo $t$ \cite{cryptoeprint:2021/204}.
