\section{Residue Number System}
\label{sec:rns}

The lattice-based schemes BFV, BGV, and CKKS all operate in a polynomial ring $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[X]/(X^N + 1)$ where $q$ is the \emph{ciphertext modulus}. To support a multiplicative depth $L$, $q$ must be on the order of $2^{60L}$ bits or more --- far larger than a 64-bit machine word. Doing arithmetic on such large numbers natively would require expensive multi-precision routines for every coefficient operation.

The \gls{rns} sidesteps this by representing each coefficient as a tuple of small residues, then performing arithmetic component-wise. The reconstruction is given by the \gls{crt}.

\subsection{The Chinese Remainder Theorem}
\label{sec:rns:crt}

\begin{theorem}[CRT]
\label{thm:crt}
Let $q_0, q_1, \dots, q_{k-1}$ be pairwise coprime moduli with $q = \prod_i q_i$. The map
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:crt-iso}
    \phi : \mathbb{Z}_q \to \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} \mathbb{Z}_{q_i}, \qquad x \mapsto ([x]_{q_0}, [x]_{q_1}, \dots, [x]_{q_{k-1}})
\end{equation}
is a ring isomorphism. The inverse is given explicitly by
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:crt-recon}
    \phi^{-1}(x_0, \dots, x_{k-1}) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} x_i \cdot \hat{q}_i \cdot [\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i} \pmod{q}, \qquad \hat{q}_i = q/q_i.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The same map lifts to polynomial rings: $R_q \cong \prod_i R_{q_i}$. The factor $q_i$ is called an \emph{RNS limb} or \emph{tower}, and $\hat{q}_i$ is the \emph{punctured product}.

For OpenFHE, each $q_i$ is a 60-bit prime (or 48-bit on AVX-512 builds) chosen to support the \gls{ntt} for the ring degree $N$. With this choice, addition and multiplication of two CRT-represented polynomials reduce to $k$ independent native-word operations per coefficient.

\subsection{CRT representation in code}
\label{sec:rns:crt-rep}

We write $\mathsf{CRT}(a) = (a^{(0)}, a^{(1)}, \dots, a^{(k-1)})$ with $a^{(i)} \in R_{q_i}$. Addition and multiplication are component-wise:
\begin{equation}
    \mathsf{CRT}(a + b) = (a^{(i)} + b^{(i)})_i, \qquad \mathsf{CRT}(a \cdot b) = (a^{(i)} \cdot b^{(i)})_i.
\end{equation}

The cost of multiplication of two degree-$N$ polynomials in $R_{q_i}$ is dominated by the convolution. Naively this is $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$, but the \gls{ntt} reduces it to $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$.

\subsection{Double-CRT representation}
\label{sec:rns:dcrt}

The \gls{ntt} is essentially a discrete Fourier transform performed modulo $q_i$. It maps the polynomial $a^{(i)} \in R_{q_i}$ from \emph{coefficient form} (a list of $N$ coefficients) to \emph{evaluation form} (a list of $N$ values, one per primitive $2N$-th root of unity in $\mathbb{Z}_{q_i}$). In evaluation form, polynomial multiplication becomes coordinate-wise multiplication --- a single point-wise loop instead of a convolution.

The \emph{Double-CRT} (DCRT) representation stores each $a^{(i)}$ in evaluation form across all towers. This is OpenFHE's \texttt{DCRTPoly} class. Conversion between coefficient and evaluation form is via forward/inverse \gls{ntt}, costing one $\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$ pass per tower.

\subsection{The auxiliary modulus and the QP basis}
\label{sec:rns:qp}

For some operations --- in particular, key switching and the gadget-based decomposition we develop in \autoref{sec:rgsw:hybrid} --- it is useful to temporarily extend the working modulus from $Q$ to $Q \cdot P$, where $P = \prod_{j} p_j$ is an auxiliary product of additional primes coprime to $Q$.

We refer to the basis $\{q_0, \dots, q_{k-1}\}$ as the \emph{$Q$-side} and $\{p_0, \dots, p_{m-1}\}$ as the \emph{$P$-side}. A polynomial $a \in R_{QP}$ is then represented by $k + m$ towers: the first $k$ in the $Q$-side, the last $m$ in the $P$-side. We write $\mathsf{sizeQ} = k$ and $\mathsf{sizeP} = m$.

Two operations between the bases are crucial:

\begin{description}
    \item[\textsf{ApproxSwitchCRTBasis} ($Q \to QP$).] Given $a \in R_Q$ in CRT form, compute the $P$-side residues by extending the basis. The exact reconstruction would require knowing $a$ as an integer in $[0, Q)$, but in practice we work with a small-norm representative (assuming $\|a\| \ll Q$) and recover the $P$-side via the formula
    \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:basis-ext}
        a^{(p_j)} = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} a^{(q_i)} \cdot [\hat{q}_i^{-1}]_{q_i} \cdot \hat{q}_i \pmod{p_j} - v \cdot Q \pmod{p_j}
    \end{equation}
    for some small correction $v$ that the approximate variant simply drops. The error is bounded by $\|a\| \cdot k / 2$, which stays well below $p_j$ for reasonable parameters \autocite{halevi2019improved}.

    \item[\textsf{ApproxModDown} ($QP \to Q$).] The inverse: given $x \in R_{QP}$, compute $\lfloor x/P \rceil \in R_Q$. Concretely, this subtracts the $P$-side (re-extended into the $Q$-side) and multiplies by $[P^{-1}]_{q_i}$ in each $Q$-tower. For BGV, an additional correction maintains correctness modulo the plaintext modulus $t$.
\end{description}

The crucial identity for what follows is:

\begin{lemma}[$P$-cancellation]
\label{lem:P-cancel}
For any $x \in R_Q$ embedded into $R_{QP}$ as $(x, 0)$ (i.e.\ $Q$-side is $x$, $P$-side is $0$),
\begin{equation}
    \mathsf{ApproxModDown}(P \cdot x) \approx x \pmod{Q},
\end{equation}
where the approximation hides the basis-extension rounding error introduced by the $P$-side correction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Multiplication by $P$ on the $Q$-side is well-defined modulo $Q$ (since $P$ is invertible there). \textsf{ApproxModDown} subtracts the basis-extended $P$-side and multiplies by $[P^{-1}]_{q_i}$, which inverts the multiplication by $P$ exactly when the $P$-side is zero --- as it is by construction.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textcolor{magenta}{This lemma is the engine driving the V2 RGSW external product: the gadget $g_j$ defined in \autoref{sec:rgsw:hybrid} packages a free factor of $P$ into each row, and \textsf{ApproxModDown} is what removes it on the way out.}
