\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chp:conclusion}

% \section{Conclusion}
This thesis set out to answer two questions about \gls{spar}. What does the protocol cost to run, and how far does it scale before it becomes impractical.

On the first question, the measured cost is substantially higher than anticipated. The cost of a round is dominated by the server's write step, which in turn is dominated by internal products, an operation absent from the cost analysis in~\cite{cryptoeprint:2025/860}. A single internal product costs roughly fourteen times an external product at our parameters (\cref{tab:bench:rgsw}). The paper's expectation of round times on the order of seconds for a hundred users is not feasible with this instantiation.

On the second question, both computation time and noise growth are limiting factors. The protocol is theoretically able to support an arbitrary amount of users, yet supporting even a small amount requires large parameters that make the protocol too slow to be practical in most applications. With a quadratic growth over the number of users $\mathcal{O}(\eta^2)$, we can extrapolate that the runtime of a few hundred users would be on the order of hours.

The positive result is that \gls{spar} can be instantiated at all from well-established \gls{fhe} primitives. The protocol runs end-to-end on standard \gls{rlwe} and \gls{rgsw} constructions in OpenFHE, with correct decryption under multi-party threshold keys. The obstacles we encountered were quantitative rather than structural, which suggests future optimizations could change the picture materially.

\section{Future Work}
After discussing how the hybrid technique could be employed in \cref{sec:results:hybrid}, we believe it is worth investigating further. We believe the mixed product can improve the runtime with no \emph{high-level} changes to the \gls{spar} construction, and quantifying this improvement concretely is a natural next step. A deeper look might also reveal more ways to replace the standard digit decomposition with the more efficient hybrid technique. 

For our implementation, AVX512 acceleration was not utilized for lack of suitable hardware (\cref{sec:avx512}). It is reasonable to expect a substantial improvement in the benchmarks from this instruction set, and since OpenFHE already supports it; AVX512 likely presents the greatest ROI for future development. % Optimizing the implementation itself around 512-bit registers and hoisting the AVX512 interaction could also present a small but worth-while improvement beyond what is provided by OpenFHE, for the same reason why our multi-threading.

Finally, we have identified the server write function as the dominant cost of \gls{spar}. One major issue is that the function must be called $\eta$ times sequentially, as each step depends on the previous ones. Any work that changes or amortizes this behavior could drastically improve the performance of \gls{spar} as a whole. If \gls{simd} packing \emph{efficiently} allows the server to evaluate $N$ rounds simultaneously, as suggested in \cref{sec:simd-packing}, the amortized cost of the protocol is improved by (up to) a factor of $N$.
