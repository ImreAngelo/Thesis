The decomposition on the powers-of-base $B$ gadget $g=(1, B, \dots, B^{\ell-1})$ from \cref{sec:hps} is base-$B$ digit \emph{extraction}, whereby the \emph{unsigned} digits become $D(u) = \{\lfloor u/B^{i} \rfloor \bmod{B}\}_{i=0}^{\ell-1} \in [0, B)^{\ell}$ with quality $\beta=B$. \Cref{rem:extprod-asymmetry} shows the noise of an external product grows with $\beta$. Centering the digits to $d_i \in [-B/2, B/2)$ decreases the bound to $\beta=B/2$. % However, this also introduces a sequential .
The residues are stored unsigned, so centering is not free: mapping a digit $d_i \geq B/2$ into the negative range subtracts $B$ from it, which must be compensated by a $+1$ carry into $d_{i+1}$. This introduces a \emph{sequential carry propagation} which prevents efficient parallelization. To fix this, we apply a well-known offset trick by adding the constant
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:carry-offset}
    \varGamma = \frac{B}{2}\cdot\frac{B^{\ell} - 1}{B - 1}
\end{equation}

%% TODO: State \varGamma must satisfy c + \varGamma < B^{\ell} 
The second fraction is the standard identity for the sum of a finite geometric series, meaning~$\varGamma$ represents the gadget decomposition of a base-$B$ number $\gamma$ where every digit is exactly $B/2$.
\begin{gather*}
    \frac{B^{\ell} - 1}{B - 1} = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} B^i = 1 + B + B^2 + \dots + B^{\ell - 1} \\
    \implies\;\; \varGamma = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{B}{2}\cdot B^i = \langle \decomp{\gamma}{}, g \rangle
\end{gather*}

To decompose a positive integer $c \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$ into signed digits $[d_i]_{B}$, the offset is applied to compute a shifted, non-negative integer $c' = c + \varGamma$. This converts signed into unsigned digit decomposition on the shifted integer $c'$, which is then shifted into the correct range by subtracting $B/2$.

        \Require Integer $c \in \mathbb{Z}$

            \State $d_i \gets u_i - half$
            \Comment{Recenter in $[-B/2, B/2)$}
            % \Comment{Replaces \autoref{alg:bit:center} in the unsigned carry case}
        \EndFor

        \Require Base $B$, decomposition parameter $\ell$, offset $\varGamma$
        \Require Integer $c \in \mathbb{Z}$
        \Ensure Array of $\ell$ signed digits $d = (d_0, d_1, \dots, d_{\ell-1}) \in R_{\beta}^{\ell}$ with quality $\beta = B/2$

As with $\Call{GetPower}{i,j}$ from \cref{alg:bv:encrypt}, the offset $\varGamma$ is calculated exactly once; when the context is first created. Because $B=2^b$ is a power of two, this extraction is performed purely with the bitwise operations of \cref{sec:bitwise}.

Once we have a working $\Call{Decompose}{}$ function, the external product can be written as \autoref{alg:external-product}. Our implementation is functionally the same, except that it has been split into three stages for better parallelization. First, the $2k$ inverse \gls{ntt} evaluations required by the decompositions are calculated in parallel, one per tower of each of the two ciphertext elements. Second, the decompose calls are merged into the external product function, and every tower of every digit is sliced, centered and transformed independently, giving $2k\ell\cdot k$ parallel tasks and as many forward \gls{ntt} calls. Finally, the inner product is accumulated over the $2k$ pairs of ciphertext component and tower, each thread summing its own $2k\ell$ terms with no reduction across threads.

The width of every stage depends on $k$ as well as $\ell$, so a parameter set with more \gls{rns} moduli exposes proportionally more parallelism. %% TODO: A more efficient implementation could utilize more threads, spread over the coefficients, increasing the upper bound on threads to N*kl

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{External Product}
\label{alg:external-product}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require RLWE ciphertext $(c_0, c_1) \in R_Q^2$, RGSW ciphertext $C \in R_Q^{2k\ell}$
\Ensure RLWE ciphertext of product
\State $acc \gets (0, 0)$
\State $d_0 \gets \Call{Decompose}{c_0}$
\State $d_1 \gets \Call{Decompose}{c_1}$
\For{$r \gets [0, k\ell)$}
    \State $acc[0] \gets acc[0] + d_0[r] \cdot C[r][0] + d_1[r]\cdot C[r + k\ell][0]$
    \State $acc[1] \gets acc[1] + d_0[r] \cdot C[r][1] + d_1[r]\cdot C[r + k\ell][1]$
\EndFor
\State \Return $acc$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
