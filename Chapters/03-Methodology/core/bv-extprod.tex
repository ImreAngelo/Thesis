The second layer of the gadget from \autoref{sec:gadget-decomposition} is a decomposition on $g = (1, B, \dots, B^{\ell-1})$, which is essentially digit extraction. Computing the digits as unsigned base-$B$ values puts each $d_i$ in $[0, B)$ and gives quality $\beta = B - 1$.

By \autoref{rem:extprod-asymmetry} the noise of an external product grows with $\beta$, so it is worth halving. Centering the digits to $d_i \in [-B/2, B/2)$ does exactly that, at $\beta = B/2$. The residues are stored unsigned, so centering is not free: mapping a digit $d_i \geq B/2$ into the negative range subtracts $B$ from it, which must be compensated by a $+1$ carry into $d_{i+1}$. Standard recoding therefore walks the digits from least to most significant, and that dependency is what prevents the integer from being sliced into independent bit-windows.

To avoid sequential carry propagation, we apply a well-known offset trick by adding the constant $\varGamma$~\eqref{eq:carry-offset}.
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:carry-offset}
    \varGamma = \frac{B}{2}\cdot\frac{B^{\ell} - 1}{B - 1}
\end{equation}

The second fraction is the standard mathematical identity for the sum of a finite geometric series, meaning~\eqref{eq:carry-offset} represents the $g$-decomposition of a base-$B$ number $\gamma$ where every digit is $B/2$.
\begin{gather*}
    \frac{B^{\ell} - 1}{B - 1} = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} B^i = 1 + B + B^2 + \dots + B^{\ell - 1} \\
    \implies\;\; \varGamma = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{B}{2}\cdot B^i = \langle \decomp{\gamma}{}, g \rangle
\end{gather*}

To decompose a positive integer $c \in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$ into signed digits $[d]_{B}$, we first apply the global offset to compute a shifted, non-negative integer $c' = c + \varGamma$. We then slice $c'$ into intermediate unsigned base-$B$ digits $u_i$. Because $B=2^b$ is a power of two, this extraction is performed purely via the independent bitwise \autoref{alg:bit:center}, completely bypassing sequential dependencies.

\begin{algorithm}[ht!]
\caption[Parallel Signed Decomposition]{\protect\Call{Decompose}{$c, b, \ell$}}
\label{alg:decomp:parallel}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require Bit-length $b$ (where $B = 2^b$), decomposition parameter $\ell$, offset $\varGamma$
    \Require Integer $c \in \mathbb{Z}$
    \Ensure Array of $\ell$ signed digits $d = (d_0, d_1, \dots, d_{\ell-1}) \in R_{\beta}^{\ell}$ with quality $\beta = B/2$
    
    \State $c' \gets c + \varGamma$ \Comment{Shift all digits to positive range [0, B)}
    \State mask $\gets \Call{LeftShift}{1, b} - 1$
    \State half $\gets \Call{LeftShift}{1, b - 1}$

    \For{$i \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $\ell - 1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
        \State $s_i \gets \Call{RightShift}{c',\; i \cdot b}$ \Comment{Remove smaller digits}
        \State $u_i \gets \Call{And}{s_i,\; \text{mask}}$ \Comment{Remove larger digits}
        \State $d_i \gets \Call{And}{u_i + \text{half},\; \text{mask}} - \text{half}$
        \Comment{\autoref{alg:bit:center}}
    \EndFor
    
    \State\Return $d = (d_0, d_1, \dots, d_{\ell-1})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

As with $\Call{GetPower}{i,j}$ from \autoref{sec:getpower} the offset $\varGamma$ is calculated exactly once, when the context is first created.\footnote{Specifically the bitwise $\varGamma = (1 \ll (b-1))\cdot((2 \ll (b \ell)) - 1)/((1\ll b) - 1)$ is used, but this is slightly less human readable than \eqref{eq:carry-offset}.} 

\newpage
Once we have a working $\Call{Decompose}{}$ function, the external product can be written as \autoref{alg:external-product}. Our implementation is functionally the same, except that it has been split into three stages for better parallelization. First, the $2k$ inverse \gls{ntt} evaluations required by the decompositions are calculated in parallel, one per tower of each of the two ciphertext elements. Second, the decompose calls are merged into the external product function, and every tower of every digit is sliced, centered and transformed independently, giving $2k\ell\cdot k$ parallel tasks and as many forward \gls{ntt} calls. Finally, the inner product is accumulated over the $2k$ pairs of ciphertext component and tower, each thread summing its own $2k\ell$ terms with no reduction across threads.

The width of every stage depends on $k$ as well as $\ell$, so a parameter set with more \gls{rns} primes exposes proportionally more parallelism.

%%%
%%% External Product
%%%
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{External Product}
\label{alg:external-product}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require RLWE ciphertext $(c_0, c_1) \in R_Q^2$, RGSW ciphertext $C \in R_Q^{2k\ell}$
\Ensure RLWE ciphertext of product
\State $acc \gets (0, 0)$
\State $d_0 \gets \Call{Decompose}{c_0}$
\State $d_1 \gets \Call{Decompose}{c_1}$
\For{$r \gets [0, k\ell)$}
    \State $acc[0] \gets acc[0] + d_0[r] \cdot C[r][0] + d_1[r]\cdot C[r + k\ell][0]$
    \State $acc[1] \gets acc[1] + d_0[r] \cdot C[r][1] + d_1[r]\cdot C[r + k\ell][1]$
\EndFor
\State \Return $acc$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
