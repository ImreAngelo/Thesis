The gadget matrix $G$ is instantiated with the double-layered gadget of \autoref{sec:gadget-decomposition}, where the HPS gadget from \eqref{def:hps:P} maps $R_Q \mapsto R_{q_0}\times\dots\times R_{q_{k-1}}$ and the power-of-$B$ gadget $P_g(v) = v\cdot g$ with $g = (1, B, \dots, B^{\ell-1})$ is applied to each limb, resulting in $k\ell$ digits.
\begin{equation}
    \label{def:bv:P}
    \bv{P}\colon R_Q \mapsto R_Q^{k\ell}, \qquad \bv{P}(v) = (P_g \circ \hps{P})(v) = \hps{P}(v) \otimes g
\end{equation}

Every element of $\bv{P}(v)$ is a fixed constant times $v$, so the message can be absorbed into the gadget term of \eqref{def:rgsw}.
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:hps:ciphertext}
    C = Z + \mu G = Z + \mu\cdot(I_2\otimes \bv{P}(1)) = Z + I_2\otimes \bv{P}(\mu)
\end{equation}
Encryption then reduces to evaluating $\bv{P}(\mu)$ and adding each of the $k\ell$ digits to two fresh encryptions of zero, once in each component.

Recall from \autoref{sec:gadget-decomposition} that the $j$-th component of $\hps{P}(\mu)$ reduces to $[\mu]_{q_j}$ in tower $j$ and vanishes in every other; so the only non-zero residues of $\bv{P}(\mu)$ is $B^i\cdot[\mu ]_{q_j} \bmod q_j = [\mu B^i]_{q_j}$. Writing the full gadget in \firstForm~\eqref{form:crt}, where row $i$ corresponds to the $q_i$-tower and each column corresponds to a full \gls{rns} polynomial, yields a \textit{sparse} matrix of $k\ell\times k$ residues rather than a dense vector in $R_Q^{k\ell}$.
\begin{gather*}
    %([\mu]_{q_0}, [\mu B]_{q_0}, \dots, [\mu B^\ell]_{q_{k-1}}) \\
    \left[ \begin{array}{ccccccccc}
        [\mu]_{q_0} & [\mu B]_{q_0} & \cdots & [\mu B^{\ell -1 }]_{q_0} & 0 & \dots & 0 & \dots & 0 \\
        0 & 0 &  \dots & 0 &[\mu]_{q_1} & \cdots & [\mu B^{\ell -1 }]_{q_{1}} &  \dots & 0 \\
        \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
        0 & 0 & \dots & 0 & 0 & \dots & 0 & \cdots & [\mu B^{\ell -1 }]_{q_{k-1}}  \\
    \end{array} \right]%^{\intercal}
\end{gather*}

We exploit the sparsity of this structure by discarding the zero residues. Since their contribution to any product is $0$, we simply skip any calculations that would have resulted in 0. Conveniently, the resulting structure is exactly the \firstForm{} of $(\mu, \mu B, \dots, \mu B^{\ell}) \in R_Q^{\ell}$. Multiplying the full polynomial $\mu$ by a scalar $B^i$ can be done natively in the evaluation domain, and the previous derivation does not change if $\mu$ is in \secondForm{}, meaning we do not need to perform any \Call{NTT}{} or \Call{InverseNTT}{} operations when encrypting.

\begin{equation*} %% TODO: Is the matrix transposed?
    {P}_{\mathsf{BV}}(\mu) \;\overset{\tiny\text{flatten}}{\longmapsto}\;
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \textcolor{hpsgreen}{[\mu]_{q_0}} & \cdots & [\mu B^{\ell - 1}]_{q_0} \\
        \textcolor{hpsgreen}{[\mu]_{q_1}} & \cdots & [\mu B^{\ell - 1}]_{q_1} \\
        \textcolor{hpsgreen}{\vdots} & \ddots & \vdots  \\
        \textcolor{hpsgreen}{[\mu]_{q_{k-1}}} & \cdots & [\mu B^{\ell - 1}]_{q_{k-1}}  \\
    \end{bmatrix}
    = (\textcolor{hpsgreen}{[\mu]_{\mathcal{B}_Q}}, \dots, [\mu B^{\ell - 1}]_{\mathcal{B}_Q})
\end{equation*}

The ciphertext still contains $2k\ell$ rows, each encoding one combination of $[\mu B^i]_{q_j}$, yet the gadget itself costs nothing to apply. Nor is there any domain-conversion penalties, as the encryption happens entirely in the evaluation domain. The memory footprint of $\Call{EncryptRGSW}{}$ is also reduced by a factor of $k$, storing only the $k\ell$ non-zero residues. Furthermore, every element of $\bv{P}(\mu)$ is independent, making the rows in~\eqref{eq:hps:ciphertext} efficiently parallelizable over $k\ell$ threads.
